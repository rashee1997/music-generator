# Valence and arousal

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| Feeling words sit on a plane with a pleasant-unpleasant axis (valence) and an activated-calm axis (arousal): Russell's circumplex of affect (1980) | from memory |
| Predicting arousal from audio features (loudness, onset rate, brightness, tempo) is easier than predicting valence; published valence regressions are much weaker | from memory |
| Mode (major against minor) carries valence in Western music; the Krumhansl-Kessler key profiles are templates of the pitch-class weights of major and minor keys | from memory; the 24 numbers in the preset are as remembered |
| Roughness and clashing pitch classes lower perceived pleasantness | from memory |
| The coordinates given to the 24 emotion words | starting values: this skill's reading of the circumplex, not published coordinates |
| The arousal composite (mean of the z-scores of loudness, onset rate and spectral centroid across sections) and the valence measure (major-minor template difference minus a clash term) | starting values, not fitted to any annotated data |

What the gate does with them: it compares the order of sections by measured arousal with the order the direction
sheet gives their energy, and the sign of the measured valence with the sign the brief's emotion words imply.
Neither is a verdict on taste. The valence row can only warn.
