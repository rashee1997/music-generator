### Procedural SFX and ambience, and blending them with music
- file: `knowledgebase/sound-design/sfx-ambience.md`
- id: `sfx-ambience`

#### 1.0 The 5 Laws of Zero-Overlap SFX-to-Music Blending
> **CRITICAL AGENT DIRECTIVE:** Raw environmental sounds (rain, wind, crowds, ocean surf, fire) are **broadband stochastic signals** spanning `20Hz to 20,000Hz`. If you simply mix them at `-12dB` underneath music, they destroy drum punch and mask lead melodies. **Every ambient or SFX track MUST pass through these 5 mathematical processors before summing with music:**

#### 1.1 LAW 1: Dynamic Spectral Notching (The "Complementarity Contract")
Divide the audible spectrum into **Protected Musical Zones** and carve them out of the Ambience/SFX layer in real time:
* **Sub-Bass Protection (`0Hz – 140Hz`):** Apply a strict **4th-order Butterworth High-Pass Filter at `140Hz`** on all continuous ambience (rain, wind, crowd, forest). *Exception:* Discrete cinematic thunder/explosion impacts, which must duck the musical bass for `300ms`.
* **Lead Formant Protection (`900Hz – 3,400Hz`):** Whenever a Lead Instrument (*Flute, Nadaswaram, Vocal, Guitar, Synth*) is active, apply a **Dynamic Notch Filter (`-8dB to -14dB`, `Q = 1.8`)** on the Ambience layer centered at the Lead's instantaneous fundamental frequency $f_0(t)$ and its 2nd harmonic $2 f_0(t)$!
* **Hi-Hat / Shaker Air Sharing (`6kHz – 14kHz`):** When `Salangai` bells, shakers, or hi-hats strike, duck the high-frequency shelf (`>6kHz`) of rain/wind by `-6dB` for `35ms` so every metallic percussion transient sparkles cleanly.

#### 1.2 LAW 2: Temporal Call-and-Response Gating ("Breathing in the Gaps")
Discrete SFX (bird calls, Kuthu street whistles, thunder cracks, wolf howls, sword clashes, water droplets) must **never trigger on top of a dense melodic phrase**:
* **The Inverse-Envelope Rule:** Compute the smoothed amplitude envelope of the musical Lead + Drums: $M_{env}(t)$.
* **SFX Trigger Probability:** $P(\text{trigger } | \text{ time } t) \propto (1.0 - M_{env}(t))^2$.
* **Result:** Birdsong, street whistles, and distant calls naturally **step forward during musical rests, intros, breakdowns, and phrase tails**, and automatically quiet down (`-10dB`) during busy musical climaxes!

#### 1.3 LAW 3: Key-Locked Harmonic Resonator Bank ("Making the Wind & Rain Sing")
Why does wind or rain in a movie score sound alive, whereas raw pink noise sounds like television static?
* **The Secret:** Pass `25% to 40%` of the raw wind, rain, or ocean noise through a **Parallel Bank of High-Q Bandpass Resonators (`Q = 25.0 to 45.0`) tuned to the exact Root, 5th, Octave, and Modal 3rd/7th of the track's current scale**!
* **Psychoacoustic Effect:** The listener's brain still hears natural wind or rain, but the air itself subtly **hums in the exact Raga / Maqam / Key of the song**, eliminating dissonance between noise and music!

#### 1.4 LAW 4: 3D Spherical Mid-Side (M/S) & Haas Depth Separation
Music and Ambience must occupy **different physical geometries** around the listener's head:
* **The Music Core (`Mid Channel` + Tight Stereo `±45°`):** Kick, Bass, Snare, and Lead Melody sit firmly in the center (`Mid`) with zero pre-delay (closest to the listener's face).
* **The Ambience Halo (`Side Channel` `±75° to ±90°` + Haas Decorrelation):**
  * Attenuate the **Mid (`L+R`) channel** of continuous ambience by **`-6dB`**, and boost its **Side (`L-R`) channel** by **`+3dB`**.
  * Apply a **7ms to 13ms All-Pass / Haas Decorrelation Delay** between Left and Right ambience channels + a `15%` wet Early-Reflection room blur.
  * **Psychoacoustic Effect:** The ambience wraps around the *outside* of the listener's ears like a 360-degree dome, leaving a crystal-clear hollow tunnel in the center for the music!

### Game sound effects: variation, impact, locomotion and interface

#### The 4 Laws of Anti-Machine-Gun Randomization
1. **Gaussian Pitch Jitter:** Pitch must not be uniform-random (which causes occasional jarring leaps). It must follow a zero-mean Gaussian distribution with standard deviation $\sigma \in [25, 55]\text{ cents}$:
   $$\Delta f = f_0 \cdot \left(2^{\frac{\Delta C}{1200}} - 1\right), \quad \Delta C \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$$
2. **Velocity-Coupled Dynamic Lowpass Filtering:** Higher-velocity hits produce more high-frequency vibrational partials. Cutoff frequency $f_c$ scales exponentially with normalized impact velocity $v \in [0.1, 1.0]$:
   $$f_c(v) = f_{\min} \cdot \left(\frac{f_{\max}}{f_{\min}}\right)^{v^{1.4}}$$
3. **Micro-Attack Timing Offset:** An attack offset between $0$ and $12\text{ ms}$ breaks mechanical phase repetition across stereo channels.
4. **Round-Robin History Exclusion:** Never repeat the same variation twice in a row ($P(\text{var}_k \mid \text{var}_{k-1} = k) = 0$).

#### 2. Combat Audio Physics & Impact Acoustics
Combat sound design requires balancing **physical realism** (transient impact mechanics) with **exaggerated psychoacoustic drama** (cinematic weight).

#### 2.1 Bladed Weapons (Slashes, Clashes & Whooshes)
- **Air Whoosh (Pre-Hit):** High-velocity air displacement modeled as swept resonant bandpass noise ($Q \approx 6.0$, center frequency sweeping exponentially from $400\text{ Hz}$ to $3200\text{ Hz}$ over $120-180\text{ ms}$).
- **Blade Ringing (Metallic Clash):** When two steel blades collide, high $Q$ modal resonances ring out at harmonic partials of the steel blade ($f_1 \approx 2.4\text{ kHz}, f_2 \approx 4.8\text{ kHz}, f_3 \approx 7.1\text{ kHz}$ with decay $T_{60} \approx 400-800\text{ ms}$).
- **Flesh Impact:** Dense low-frequency impact transient ($60-120\text{ Hz}$, $35\text{ ms}$) coupled with a brief granular burst of high-frequency wet noise ($2.0-6.0\text{ kHz}$, $80\text{ ms}$).

#### 2.2 Blunt Weapons (Body Resonance & Heavy Impacts)
- **Sub-Bass Thud:** A fast exponential sine drop ($120\text{ Hz} \rightarrow 35\text{ Hz}$ over $60\text{ ms}$) providing physical weight that hits the player's chest on subwoofers.
- **Wood / Bone Snap:** High-frequency, sharp transient spike ($< 3\text{ ms}$, crest factor $> 18\text{ dB}$, bandpassed at $1.8-4.2\text{ kHz}$).
- **Body Thump:** Formant-filtered noise burst centered at $280\text{ Hz}$ with $Q = 4.0$.

#### 2.3 Projectiles, Lasers & Spells
- **Sci-Fi Laser:** Fast downward linear or exponential FM chirp:
  $$f(t) = f_{\text{start}} \cdot e^{-k \cdot t}, \quad f_{\text{start}} = 3500\text{ Hz} \rightarrow 200\text{ Hz in } 90\text{ ms}$$
- **Energy Charge:** Upward frequency sweep coupled with a $12\text{ Hz}$ amplitude-modulated sinusoidal shimmer.
- **Bullet Flyby (Whiz):** High-speed Doppler whoosh passing within centimeters of the listener's ear with instant frequency inversion.

#### 3. Player Locomotion & Terrain Foley Physics
Footsteps are the most frequently triggered sound in video games. They must convey character weight, armor class, movement speed, and exact terrain material.

## Decisions taken from this research

Weather and wipers (the user's change)
- The road is wet after rain and nothing is falling: no rain bed, no rain on awnings, no wipers. The wet is carried by tyre hiss and spray (3-4 dB(A) louder than a dry road), by a water-film hiss that rises with speed, and by short drips off the fronts at the quiet moments.

Camera and the room (as scenes, none as a script)
- Five shots: inside the car (a very short cabin room), roadside (a fixed listener on the kerb: the car passes with Doppler), chase (beside and behind the car), helicopter (far, high, filtered by air absorption, with a rotor thump) and the underpass (a long tail, about 2.5 s). Each shot is a space scene anchored to a section; the listener position and the room change at the cut.
- The pass-by Doppler is the formula above applied by the trajectory's `doppler_strength`; the helicopter's far level and top loss come from distance and the ISO 9613-1 air model with 10 C and 95 % humidity declared in the score.
