### Hybrid orchestral film and trailer scoring - tension, pressure, loudness and sample realism
- file: `knowledgebase/genre-and-feel/cinematic-hybrid-scoring.md`
- id: `cinematic-hybrid-scoring`

#### 1. Pressure, not mood
A calm cue and a dangerous one can share a key, a tempo and an orchestra. They differ in how much of the timeline is
under load. Four things carry the load; a cue that has none of them reads as ambient however dark its chords are.
| Carrier | What it is | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| A pulse that does not stop | a short repeated figure in low strings or percussion, made of notes of the sounding chord, with a few notes changed on each repeat so the shape stays and the pitches move | per search result: an analysis of repeated figures found them mostly chord tones, tweaked on each repeat |
| Low brass and sub weight | very low, very loud brass tones as the "large" sound of the cue | per search result: the low brass blast became a trailer staple after 2010, with precedents in 2007 and 2009 trailers |
| Percussion that is constant | drums as a continuous layer, with accents from trombones and low string staccatos played with the drums | per search result: trombones add accents, often as staccatos beside percussion and low strings |
| A rising or accelerating device | a pitch that seems to climb for ever, or a pulse that speeds up | per search result: a stack of octave-spaced tones moved up in pitch gives the impression of endless ascent; a ticking pulse gives a metric spine |
A cue where the loudest stretch is a few seconds long, and where whole sections drop to a whisper, has pressure only
in those seconds. `measured here`: a 97 s cue written to be tense had its loudest 60 s sit at -13 to -18 dBFS RMS with
dips to -31 dBFS RMS, and was heard by its brief's author as meditative.

#### 2. Tension devices by instrument
| Device | Effect | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Pedal point (a held bass note under changing chords) | even very dissonant chords stay coherent over it; good for builds | per search result |
| Tremolo (rapid repeated or alternating notes) | tension from escalation: more instruments, louder or more regular repeats, pitches rising step by step | per search result |
| Cluster (adjacent pitches together) | a dark cloud; it needs inner movement or it reads as a mass of clashing partials | per search result |
| Bowing near the bridge | an icy, unstable colour with more high overtones | per search result |
| Slow glissando | feels more menacing than a fast one because the pitch change is drawn out | per search result |
| Dissonance that does not resolve | ambiguity is what the listener reads as threat | per search result |
Roughness is the measurable side of dissonance: a cue can be tense and still pass `cochlear_roughness` if the
clashing pair is not sustained. See `knowledgebase/sound-design/roughness.md`.

#### 3. Shape of a trailer-style cue
| Part | What happens | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Open, lull, build, cut to black, final hit | the usual order; the lull is deliberate, because a cue that is exciting throughout is not exciting | per search result |
| Silence before the last hit | a sudden drop to near silence just before the final impact makes the impact larger by contrast | per search result |
| A small kit | a tension drone, a riser, a whoosh, a big hit and a sub drop are enough for the build-and-release shape | per search result |
| A hit followed by silence | the listener supplies what was not shown | per search result |

#### 4. Loudness and dynamics
The sources disagree and none is a standard. Treat the figures as the range to measure against.
| Figure | Where | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| -14 LUFS for music mastered for film, the video editor expected to lower it | film and video mastering guide | per search result |
| -14 to -10 LUFS integrated for stems mastered for TV and film | same guide | per search result |
| about -8 to -10 LUFS for trailer music, with peaks allowed higher in the finale; -10 to -14 LUFS for game music | a coaching-call summary, secondary | per search result, weakest source |
| trailer and library music should not carry a large dynamic range; film and TV music may keep a wide one | mastering engineers | per search result |
| -16 LUFS integrated, 13 LU loudness range, 17.9 dB crest | one cue rendered by this skill and judged too calm | measured here |
A chart that listed trailer mixes at -20 to -24 LUFS and theatrical mixes at -27 to -31 LUFS was found and not used:
it was unsourced and the theatrical figure looks wrong. Delivery specs of the platform or trailer house override all
of the above.
Low-end control is where orchestral and hybrid mixes usually fail: a multi-band compressor that narrows the lower
bands is the tool one engineer named (per search result). In this skill that is a mix-preset decision.

#### 2. What the SF3 format is
| Finding | Basis |
|---|---|
| SF3 is an unofficial extension of SF2, made for MuseScore, that stores samples as Ogg Vorbis; a flag bit in the sample header marks it | per search result (FluidSynth wiki, Polyphone) |
| It is much smaller (a 148 MB Fluid soundfont became 20 MB) and lossy, so unsuited to archiving; there is no formal specification | per search result |
| MuseScore General is MIT-licensed, 206 MB as SF2 and 38 MB as SF3; it is a general-MIDI bank, not an orchestral library | per search result (simpleSoundbank) |
| The `tinysoundfont` package listing says it loads SF2, SF3 and SFO files | per search result (PyPI listing; not loaded in this repository) |

#### 3. Orchestral realism, from library documentation and tutorials
| Finding | Basis |
|---|---|
| Velocity shapes how a note begins; CC1 and CC11 shape its evolution. CC11 is usually volume and CC1 usually crossfades dynamic layers | per search result (Soundfly, Sonarworks; the mapping depends on the library) |
| Each part on its own MIDI channel lets each player have its own controller moves | per search result |
| Combine articulations (legato for a line, staccato for rhythm); add timing humanisation | per search result |
| Reverb is treated as the most important effect; convolution reverb is preferred for an orchestral space; reverb narrows the dynamic range of a dry patch | per search result (Production Expert) |
| No free SF2 with confirmed round robins was found; a community SF2 conversion of Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra exists but the original is SFZ | per search result (simpleSoundbank) |

## Web research

- **Karanyi Sounds, "Cinematic Tension Music: How to Write a Tension Cue"** (https://karanyisounds.com/blogs/production-tips/cinematic-tension-music-cue)
  Takeaway: chase cues sit at 120-140 BPM and the bar line should land on the cut; a hit on the downbeat reads as
  intentional, half a beat late as a mistake. Used: tempo 140 and every section boundary on a bar line.
- **Evenant, "Film Scoring: Introduction to Action Cues"** (https://www.evenant.com/articles/film-scoring-introduction-to-action-cues)
  Takeaway: action cues work through high rhythmic activity, frequent modulation and fragmented melodies, with
  dissonant harmony. Used: a two-bar cell fragmented across the peaks, Dm -> Eb -> F -> Gm modulation, and the A major
  dominant that never resolves.
- **MusicTech, "Action Scoring with Drums"** (https://musictech.com/tutorials/logic-tutorial-action-scoring)
  Takeaway: cinematic percussion is three layers - high-end movement, mid toms/taiko for drive, low taiko/bass drum for
  weight - and the low layer must not be frenetic or the bottom turns to mud. Used: 16th high snare/cymbal movement,
  8th taiko mid layer, bass drum and timpani on beats 1 and 3 only.
- **Berklee Online, "The Movie Score: Lessons from 5 Classic Films"** (https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/film-scoring-tips-from-5-classic-movies)
  Takeaway: in effect-heavy scenes (car chases) percussion that competes with the effects can be limited, and driving
  strings and brass often carry the cue better; the music must match the cut pacing. Used: the ostinato and the horn
  cell carry the peaks; the percussion is a floor, not a soloist.
- **F. Lehman, "Manufacturing the Epic Score" (2016)** (https://franklehman.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2016-Manufacturing-the-Epic-Score-Music-Epic-Film-Routledge-1.pdf)
  Takeaway: the Zimmer chase cue (Batman Begins, "Molossus") sustains urgency with a constant ostinato under a
  long-breathed melody; the melody is what makes the cue more than a loop. Used: the horn cell over a 16th ostinato,
  with the cell inverted rather than repeated literally in the second peak.
- **AmbiX format (Nachbar, Zotter, Deleflie & Sontacchi, 2011)** (already cited by `references/data/presets/outputs/ambix.json`)
  Takeaway: ACN channel order, SN3D normalisation; a fifth-order field is 36 channels. Used: the `ambix` output target
  (order 5), delivered beside the stereo master.

## Decisions taken from this research

1. Tempo 140 BPM, 35 bars, 60.000 s: every camera cut lands on a bar line (Karanyi, Berklee on cut pacing).
2. The cell (root, minor 3rd, 2nd, 5th) on four horns is the hook; it is fragmented in the build, stated in peak 1 and
   inverted in the flyover (Evenant, Lehman).
3. Three percussion layers with the low layer on beats 1 and 3 only (MusicTech); no percussion solo, so the chase's
   effects have room (Berklee).
4. Harmony: pedal D under Dm | Bb | Gm | A, the Neapolitan Eb at both peaks, no resolution of the dominant until the
   button (Evenant's "dissonant, unstable" action harmony).
5. Rooms change with the camera, not with a reverb preset switch: open highway, close cockpit, tunnel bore, flyover
   deck and a wide near-miss, all geometry + materials + late field in `presets/spaces/highway_canyon.json`.
6. Deliverables: stereo (certified master) and `ambix` order 5 (36 channels) beside it.
