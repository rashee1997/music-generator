#### 1.0 The 5 Laws of Zero-Overlap SFX-to-Music Blending
> **CRITICAL AGENT DIRECTIVE:** Raw environmental sounds (rain, wind, crowds, ocean surf, fire) are **broadband stochastic signals** spanning `20Hz to 20,000Hz`. If you simply mix them at `-12dB` underneath music, they destroy drum punch and mask lead melodies. **Every ambient or SFX track MUST pass through these 5 mathematical processors before summing with music:**

#### 1.1 LAW 1: Dynamic Spectral Notching (The "Complementarity Contract")
Divide the audible spectrum into **Protected Musical Zones** and carve them out of the Ambience/SFX layer in real time:
* **Sub-Bass Protection (`0Hz – 140Hz`):** Apply a strict **4th-order Butterworth High-Pass Filter at `140Hz`** on all continuous ambience (rain, wind, crowd, forest). *Exception:* Discrete cinematic thunder/explosion impacts, which must duck the musical bass for `300ms`.
* **Lead Formant Protection (`900Hz – 3,400Hz`):** Whenever a Lead Instrument (*Flute, Nadaswaram, Vocal, Guitar, Synth*) is active, apply a **Dynamic Notch Filter (`-8dB to -14dB`, `Q = 1.8`)** on the Ambience layer centered at the Lead's instantaneous fundamental frequency $f_0(t)$ and its 2nd harmonic $2 f_0(t)$!
* **Hi-Hat / Shaker Air Sharing (`6kHz – 14kHz`):** When `Salangai` bells, shakers, or hi-hats strike, duck the high-frequency shelf (`>6kHz`) of rain/wind by `-6dB` for `35ms` so every metallic percussion transient sparkles cleanly.

#### 1.2 LAW 2: Temporal Call-and-Response Gating ("Breathing in the Gaps")
Discrete SFX (bird calls, Kuthu street whistles, thunder cracks, wolf howls, sword clashes, water droplets) must **never trigger on top of a dense melodic phrase**:
* **The Inverse-Envelope Rule:** Compute the smoothed amplitude envelope of the musical Lead + Drums: $M_{env}(t)$.
* **SFX Trigger Probability:** $P(\text{trigger } | \text{ time } t) \propto (1.0 - M_{env}(t))^2$.
* **Result:** Birdsong, street whistles, and distant calls naturally **step forward during musical rests, intros, breakdowns, and phrase tails**, and automatically quiet down (`-10dB`) during busy musical climaxes!

#### 1.3 LAW 3: Key-Locked Harmonic Resonator Bank ("Making the Wind & Rain Sing")
Why does wind or rain in a movie score sound alive, whereas raw pink noise sounds like television static?
* **The Secret:** Pass `25% to 40%` of the raw wind, rain, or ocean noise through a **Parallel Bank of High-Q Bandpass Resonators (`Q = 25.0 to 45.0`) tuned to the exact Root, 5th, Octave, and Modal 3rd/7th of the track's current scale**!
* **Psychoacoustic Effect:** The listener's brain still hears natural wind or rain, but the air itself subtly **hums in the exact Raga / Maqam / Key of the song**, eliminating dissonance between noise and music!

#### 1.4 LAW 4: 3D Spherical Mid-Side (M/S) & Haas Depth Separation
Music and Ambience must occupy **different physical geometries** around the listener's head:
* **The Music Core (`Mid Channel` + Tight Stereo `±45°`):** Kick, Bass, Snare, and Lead Melody sit firmly in the center (`Mid`) with zero pre-delay (closest to the listener's face).
* **The Ambience Halo (`Side Channel` `±75° to ±90°` + Haas Decorrelation):**
  * Attenuate the **Mid (`L+R`) channel** of continuous ambience by **`-6dB`**, and boost its **Side (`L-R`) channel** by **`+3dB`**.
  * Apply a **7ms to 13ms All-Pass / Haas Decorrelation Delay** between Left and Right ambience channels + a `15%` wet Early-Reflection room blur.
  * **Psychoacoustic Effect:** The ambience wraps around the *outside* of the listener's ears like a 360-degree dome, leaving a crystal-clear hollow tunnel in the center for the music!

#### 1.5 LAW 5: Rhythmic Quantization of Micro-Foley (Groove-Locked Nature)
When blending transient nature/world sounds (fire crackles, water drops, footsteps, horse hooves, crickets, café cup clinks, handclaps) with rhythmic music:
* **Do NOT place them at 100% random timestamps!** Random transients fight the drum groove and make the beat sound sloppy.
* **The Soft-Quantize Rule (`75% Grid Attraction`):** Snap micro-foley transients to within `±15ms` of the track's **16th-note or 12th-note (triplet) subdivisions**! When raindrops, fire pops, or footsteps subtly lock into the pocket of a Lo-Fi, Kuthu, or Celtic beat, the human brain experiences intense rhythmic satisfaction.

#### 1. Resonator: modal bank
Response of an object to an impulse at location k (paper text read; van den Doel, Kry, Pai, *FoleyAutomatic*, SIGGRAPH 2001, eq. 1):
    y_k(t) = sum_n  a_nk * exp(-d_n * t) * sin(2 pi f_n t),     t >= 0
`f_n` are mode frequencies, `d_n` decay rates (1/s), `a_nk` gains that depend on where the object is struck. Frequencies and dampings come
from geometry and material; the gains from the mode shapes. Only the gains change with strike position.
Decay time to -60 dB: `T60_n = 6.91 / d_n`. A material usually damps high modes faster: `d_n = d_0 + d_1 * f_n` (standard).
Mode-frequency ratios (standard; check in a project's research for the exact object):
| Object | Ratios f_n / f_1 |
|---|---|
| Ideal string, tube open at both ends | 1, 2, 3, 4, ... |
| Tube closed at one end | 1, 3, 5, 7, ... |
| Free-free bar (xylophone bar, rod, metal strip) | 1, 2.757, 5.404, 8.933, 13.34 |
| Circular membrane (drum head) | 1, 1.594, 2.136, 2.296, 2.653, 2.918 |
| Glass, bell, plate | inharmonic: take the ratios from a measured recording (`ambience_reference.py measure`), not from a law |

#### 2. Excitation
| Excitation | Formula | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Impact pulse | force `F(t) = 1 - cos(2 pi t / tau)` for `0 <= t <= tau`, else 0. `tau` is the contact time: a hard contact has a short `tau`, a soft one a long `tau`. The magnitude is the energy transferred. The resonator is driven by this force. | paper text read (FoleyAutomatic section 4.1). The paper names Hertz contact as the more detailed model; it is not used here |
| Scraping and sliding | Surface profile = fractal noise with power spectrum proportional to `omega^beta`, passed through a reson (resonant) filter whose centre frequency scales with contact velocity. Roughness `beta` relates to fractal dimension `D = beta / 2 + 2`. A narrow reson sounds pitched; a wide one sounds random. | paper text read (FoleyAutomatic, scraping). The sign convention of `beta` follows the paper (1/f^beta noise is the common reading; a project checks it by measuring the spectrum slope) |
| Rolling | A gammatone filter (order 2) driven by noise has the same spectrum as a reson driven by noise with envelope `S(omega) = 1 / sqrt((omega - rho)^2 + d^2)`, where `rho`, `d` are the mode frequency and damping. Better than a pure modal bank for rolling. | paper text read (FoleyAutomatic, rolling, citing an earlier observation) |
| Source-filter view of scraping and rolling | Excitation (roughness, speed) and resonator (object) are separate parts, so any excitation can drive any resonator. | Agarwal et al., DAFx 2021, abstract and the overlapping text read |
| Noise with a spectral slope | white, pink (-3 dB/oct), brown (-6 dB/oct), or band-limited | standard |
| Turbulence | noise multiplied by a slow flow envelope | standard |
| Periodic pulse train | saw or pulse at `f0` with jitter and a pitch glide | standard |
| Burst | short broadband pulse with a fast exponential decay (blast, gunshot) | standard |
Warning from the same paper: one model for too wide a category (from contact sounds to animal cries) gave poor sounds. This is why the engine
is a set of small parts chosen per sound, and why speech, singing and named animal calls stay with their own primitives.

#### 4. Parameter laws
| Law | Formula | Status |
|---|---|---|

| Rain noise on roofs | building-acoustics models cover 100 to 5000 Hz | search snippet of Griffin 2012, not read in full |
| Speed to level and brightness | level in dB rises with speed, and the filter cutoff rises with speed | standard |

#### 6. Texture statistics (the target for any ambience)
McDermott and Simoncelli, *Neuron* 71, 2011 (paper text read). The model:
1. a bank of band-pass ("cochlear") filters, 32 channels on a log-frequency axis, gives sub-band signals;
2. the envelope of each, with a compressive nonlinearity, gives `s_k(t)`;
3. statistics of the envelopes: the first four normalized moments: mean `M1`, variance divided by squared mean `M2`, skew `M3`, kurtosis `M4`;
   variance, skew and kurtosis measure **sparsity** (infrequent events that make bursts);
4. cross-band envelope correlations `C1` (broadband events such as crackles and claps raise them);
5. the envelopes are filtered by a bank of modulation filters (log-spaced); the power in each modulation band `M` is a statistic;
6. correlations across modulation bands `C2` (for example, onsets that are common to several bands).
Per-band statistics alone failed to give convincing textures; the cross-band correlations were needed. The engine measures a reduced version
(about 12 bands, moments, C1, a few modulation bands) so that it runs on a CPU laptop, and says plainly that it is a subset of the paper's set.

## Decisions taken from this research

Music
- Key C minor, 4/4, a tempo map from 128 to 152 BPM across the chase (a rising device that never stops), a 180 s cue.
- The pulse is a low-string and piano figure of sixteenths from the avenue to the roadblock; a heartbeat of timpani and bass drum under it; muted low brass, high-string tremolo and tritone and minor-second clusters carry the tension.
- The music stays out of the siren's lane (600-1500 Hz): brass stabs low, strings high, the siren's own pitch is kept off the key's semitone neighbours so it does not beat against the strings.

Sound (all as sound-model or scene presets, none as a script)
- The hero engine is a periodic pulse train whose f0 follows rpm x 8 / 120 through the gears (idle 800 rpm = 53 Hz, redline about 7000 rpm = 467 Hz) with body formants; a shift is a pitch step by the gear ratio and a short throttle lift.
- The drift squeal is friction with a falling friction-velocity law, fundamental 900-1400 Hz with harmonics, level and slip following the steering; wet grip is 20 % below dry.
- Two sirens: wail 600-1400 Hz at 20 cycles per minute, yelp 700-1500 Hz at 240, six harmonics; a hi-lo of 435/580 Hz for the second car so the two never share a pitch.
- Wipers at 70 cycles per minute; rain at 30 mm/h on asphalt (raised from 12 after a render: at 12 mm/h the drops were sparse enough that the bed varied by up to 10 dB between half-second blocks, which the ambient_bed row reads as pumping; 30 mm/h is still under the ISO 10140 heavy-rain figure of about 40) and on metal awnings; a far traffic bed; the crash is an impact on a damped plate plus the glass_break model.
- Slow motion at the drift: the stems that carry it (engine, squeal, music pulse) get `pitch_glide` down, `tape_stop` into a `freeze`, the rain is thinned (a lower rate and longer drops, not pitched), then `reverse_swell` and a sub drop snap it back. Water does not pitch down well, so it is not pitched.

Place and street objects (as spaces, scenes and propagation, none as a script)
- The street canyon: parallel facades of brick and glass (reflection 0.9, absorption 0.1), wet asphalt ground, a long lateral flutter.
- Parked trucks and a building corner are occluders on the siren sources (the siren dulls behind the corner and opens when the line of sight returns); a bridge undercroft and a short tunnel are room scenes (rt60 about 2.5 s) that fade in over bars.
- Air at 10 C and 95 % humidity for the absorption (ISO 9613-1), so the far sirens lose their top.
- Moving sources use trajectories with Doppler from the 7.1 bus; the bass and drums are anchors and do not travel.

Limits stated up front
- The sounds are synthesised from physical models and the sources above, not recordings; nothing was measured against a real car, siren or rain recording; every number marked ESTIMATE is a starting value.
